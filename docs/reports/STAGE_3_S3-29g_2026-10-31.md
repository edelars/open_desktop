# Stage 3 S3-29g: cached NativeRegisterV1 dispatcher comparison

Status: three release-process measurements completed on the current host after
the independent source/method review. This fixed register profile compares the
existing eager and demand-flags-candidate cached dispatch entrypoints. It does
not choose a global default or satisfy the complete Stage 3 performance gate.
Mini and physical-x86 runs remain deferred to the end of Stage 3.

## Measurement protocol

Both variants use one `SingleEntryBlockCache<PublishedCode>` entry and the
real `PublishedBlockExecutor`. Cold input resets state and constructs an empty
cache before each guest call, with the full dispatcher call timed; the cache
and its executable mapping are dropped before the next call. A bounded loop
can produce cache hits after its initial cold miss. Resident input first
prewarms one entry, then times reset guest calls against that same block. Every
resident sample has at least one hit per guest call and zero measured misses,
compiles, publications, or replacements. The resident mapping is dropped
before publishing the paired strategy.

Each case and mode has 20 paired samples. Eager-first and candidate-first
orders alternate, both variants use identical inputs and call counts, and
every measured aggregate is at least 200 ms. Samples below that floor restart
the paired series with a larger common call count. Three release processes
ran sequentially. Reset, CodeView construction, cache construction, prewarm,
validation, result serialization, and mapping drop are outside timed call
intervals. Cold timing includes lookup, compile/publication, execution,
redispatch, and any NOP interpreter fallback. Resident timing includes lookup,
native execution, redispatch, and any NOP interpreter fallback.

Across the 21 per-case/process median paired ratios, cold candidate/eager
ranges from 1.0354 to 1.1682. Resident candidate/eager ranges from 0.9993 to
1.0090. The candidate artifacts are 28.2% to 57.5% larger in these four
compiler shapes. These measurements show a clear difference between
first-dispatch compile/publication and resident execution for this profile;
they do not establish a general speedup or slowdown for other workloads.
