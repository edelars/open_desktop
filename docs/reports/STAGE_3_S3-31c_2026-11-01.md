# S3-31c — run-local prepared-key reuse

**Status:** bounded synthetic profile complete; S3-31 and Stage 3 remain open.

## Scope

Commit `dcd1bfe5db813a2647c100c4c64a008dca9bd21c` (`Prototype RUN-local native key reuse`) adds the opt-in candidate. The profile compares the ordinary cached NativeRegisterV1 dispatcher, the prepared-key candidate, and the synchronous interpreter on two fixed synthetic loops (`a` and `b`) with 1, 64, or 4,096 iterations. The candidate prepares one key per run and reuses it within that run; the candidate memo setup/use/drop is included in the timed dispatcher call.

The release preflight is a correctness-only artifact (`timing_started: false`). For all six strata, baseline, candidate, and interpreter agree on guest state, retirement, and remaining budget. Native and interpreter API exit labels differ. Each candidate case records one prepared key and `iterations - 1` reuse on the main path. Additional partial/control cases compare state, retirement, and budget after LEA, after XCHG, before JNZ, STOP before XCHG, STOP at JNZ, and a wrapping operand; reuse is absent when STOP prevents a later call and is counted separately for the wrapping case. Preflight cache counters include setup/replacement activity; scored timing samples are hit-only.

## Timed evidence

Three release processes collected 20 paired triples per stratum (60 per stratum, 360 total). Every measured baseline, candidate, and interpreter side exceeded 200 ms; the minimum of 1,080 sides was 223,746,544 ns. The measured interval covers a full synchronous interpreter call, ordinary cached dispatcher call, or prepared-key candidate call. State reset and CodeView/control setup are outside the interval; candidate memo setup/use/drop is inside it. The retained pairs and per-process summaries are in the raw JSON artifacts and suite manifest.

Ratios are candidate elapsed time divided by the comparator; below 1 means the candidate was faster. The table reports medians and raw min–max ranges across all 60 paired samples per stratum:

| Stratum | Candidate / baseline | Candidate / interpreter | Resident entry: emitted / logical bytes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `a_1` | 1.1998 [0.8821, 1.3680] | 4.7640 [3.5393, 5.0147] | 1,408 / 7,193 |
| `a_64` | 0.3532 [0.2257, 0.5166] | 1.4814 [1.0263, 2.2681] | 1,408 / 7,193 |
| `a_4096` | 0.3416 [0.3310, 0.3520] | 1.4330 [1.3094, 1.5237] | 1,408 / 7,193 |
| `b_1` | 1.1352 [1.0082, 1.1870] | 3.7066 [3.0948, 4.6654] | 2,188 / 10,552 |
| `b_64` | 0.2727 [0.2549, 0.2876] | 0.9184 [0.8491, 0.9749] | 2,188 / 10,552 |
| `b_4096` | 0.2591 [0.2428, 0.2760] | 0.8741 [0.8217, 0.9217] | 2,188 / 10,552 |

At the one-iteration median, the candidate is slower than the ordinary dispatcher in both forms; the `a_1` raw range crosses parity. At 64 and 4,096 iterations it is faster than the ordinary dispatcher in both forms. It remains slower than the interpreter across all `a` strata and `b_1`; only `b_64` and `b_4096` have candidate/interpreter ranges entirely below 1. These are results for six synthetic recurrence strata only. They do not establish a broad workload speedup, a production default or cache-policy change, or broad Stage 3 acceptance. The candidate remains opt-in.
