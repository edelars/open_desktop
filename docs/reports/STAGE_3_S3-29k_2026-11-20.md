benchmark. The host was an Apple M1 Pro with macOS 14.6.1 and Rust 1.99.0.
The build used stock `8cdac54`, the reviewed retained-flags overlay and
macOS 14.0 as the deployment target. The binary SHA-256 was
`d524fff5583280ad67f46b0508dfce2c126714e9cdd3cf7e7fac649cad5f11bc`.

## Timed work and resources

The signed untimed preflight passed all seven fixtures per strategy
against the interpreter. The timer summed the full public
`run_multi_cached_native_register_v1_controlled` invocations on the
four retained writer-replacement, partial-writer, preserving-LEA and
back-edge cases. Each sweep made four calls and recorded 19 cache
hits and 41 native retirements in 19 blocks. Misses, compilations,
publications, evictions and interpreter retirements were zero in
every timed sweep. Preparation, prewarm, reset, checks and teardown
ran outside the timer.

## Publication proof and replay

The first controller stopped during audit. Its cross-process
comparison treated absolute helper addresses in the generated words
as stable. The actual native preflight and the timed process had
14 differing publication hashes at the same ordered case positions.
The rest of the eager and demand gates matched. The first attempt
remains diagnostic with an incomplete receipt.

Both runs used the same signed executable. In each process the Rust
benchmark hashes the actual bytes handed to publication and compares
them with a direct compiler probe for that strategy. The independently
reviewed v2 controller validates and preserves the raw publication
hashes and compares all stable gate fields across processes. Fresh
v2 preflight and scoring receipts bind the binary, sources and run
approval, including the host-load and process-priority checks.

Restore the archive's UTF-8 `files` at their relative paths and verify
their hashes against `file_sha256`. The following replay reproduces
the combined audit byte for byte.
